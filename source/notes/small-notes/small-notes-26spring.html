One can define a LA (Lie algebra) as a v.s. 𝐿 with shifts 𝜏 :(𝑥,𝑦) →(𝑦,𝑥), 𝜉 :(𝑥,𝑦,𝑧) →(𝑦,𝑧,𝑥) and a map 𝜙 :𝐿 ⊗𝐿 →𝐿 s.t.

  • ker⁡(1 −𝜏) ⊂ker⁡𝜙 or 𝜙 = −𝜙 ∘𝜏
  • 𝜙 ∘(id ⊗𝜙) ∘(id +𝜉 +𝜉2) =0.

So similarly, define a LCA (Lie coalgebra) as a v.s. 𝑋 with a map 𝑑 :𝑋 →𝑋 ⊗𝑋 s.t.

  • im 𝑑 ⊂im⁡(1 −𝜏) or 𝑑 = −𝜏 ∘𝑑

[GM91: extremal length geometry]

(the main result of this paper concerns some uniqueness and the "midpoint mapping" which helps to show the cell structures of level sets of extremal length)

Let 𝑆 be an orientable surface (possibly with finite many punctures/distinguished points), T(𝑆) the Teichmüller space of 𝑆, and S the set of free homotopy classes of simple closed curves (not trivial or homotopic to the punctures) on 𝑆.

Definition (ahlfors beruling)

Define the extremal length of a family of paths Γ on 𝑅 by Ext𝑅⁡(Γ) =sup𝐿𝜌(Γ)2𝐴(𝜌), where the sup is over all conformal metric 𝜌, 𝐴(𝜌) =∫𝑅𝜌2d⁡𝑥d⁡𝑦 and 𝐿𝜌(Γ) =inf∫𝛾𝜌|d⁡𝑧|.

Definition

A measured foliation |d⁡𝑣| on 𝑆 is a foliation of 𝑆 with a transverse measure (given in a way). The leaves are somehow given by ∫Im⁡(𝑤𝑘/2d⁡𝑤) =𝑣 =const. The distinguished points where 𝑘 ≠0 are called the singularities of the m.l., and there are 𝑘 +2 trajectories meeting at the singularity (prongs/critical leaves).

For any measured foliation, we define the intersection with curves (or the height function) by 𝑖(|d⁡𝑣|,𝛾)=ℎ𝑡𝑣[𝛾]:=inf𝛾1∼𝛾ℎ𝑡𝑣(𝛾1)=inf𝛾1∼𝛾∫𝛾1|d⁡𝑣|.This gives a map MF →ℝS+. Two m.l. |d⁡𝑣|,|d⁡𝑢| are equivalent if the height functions are identical.

Remark

As described by Thurston, two m.f. are equivalent if one can be obtained from the other by a composition of mappings isotopic to the identity which preserve leaves and the transverse measure, and by a finite sequence of Whitehead moves (collapse a saddle connection) and their inverses. These two notions are identical, see this on page 110, or the english version on page 92.

A main focus is to study the GM boundary, and the convergence (in direction) of (almost-) geodesic rays.

Definition

A map 𝛾 :𝐵 →(𝑀,𝑑) where 𝐵 ⊂ℝ+ is unbounded and contains 0 is called an almost-geodesic ray if ∀𝜀 >0,∃𝑚 ⩾0 with |𝑑(𝛾(0),𝛾(𝑠))+𝑑(𝛾(𝑠),𝛾(𝑡))−𝑡|<𝜀,∀𝑚⩽𝑠⩽𝑡.

(25Rieffel) Every almost-geodesic ray of (𝑀,𝑑) converges to a point in 𝑀(∞).

Every Teichmüller (almost-) geodesic ray converges to a point in the GM boundary.

In contrast, there exists some Teichmüller geodesic ray that does not converge in Thurston's boundary[11Lenzhen].

A horofunction is called a Busemann point if it is a limit point of some almost-geodesic ray. When 3𝑔 −3 +𝑛 ⩾2, non-Busemann points of the GM boundary were constructed in [20] by Miyachi.
