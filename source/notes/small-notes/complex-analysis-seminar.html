branched coverings

Suppose 𝑓(𝑧) =𝑤0 +𝑐(𝑧 −𝑧0)𝑛 +(higher terms), where 𝑛 ⩾1, then

  • 𝑛 =𝑛(𝑧0) is the local degree of 𝑓 at 𝑧0
  • 𝑧0 is a critical point iff 𝑛 ⩾2
  • if 𝑛 ⩾2, then 𝑤0 =𝑓(𝑧0) is called a ramified point (similar to critical value).

Consider holomorphic map 𝑝 :𝑆′ →𝑆 between Riemann surfaces. 𝑝 is proper if the inverse image of any compact 𝐾 ⊂𝑆 is compact in 𝑆′.

Definition.

𝑝 is called a branched covering if for any 𝑎 ∈𝑆, there is a neighborhood 𝑈 s.t. every connected component of 𝑝−1(𝑈) maps onto 𝑈 by a proper map.

A branched covering is regular if there is a group Γ <Aut⁡(𝑆′), s.t. 𝑝(𝑧1) =𝑝(𝑧2) iff there is some 𝛾 ∈Γ with 𝛾(𝑧1) =𝑧2. In this case, 𝑆 ≅𝑆′/Γ and Γ is the group of deck transforms.

For regular branched covering 𝑝 :𝑆′ →𝑆

  • there are only finite many critical points, and they are isolated
  • each ramified point is isolated (recall that 𝑝 is an open map)
  • the local degree depends only on the image, i.e. 𝑛(𝑧1) =𝑛(𝑧2) if 𝑓(𝑧1) =𝑓(𝑧2).

ramifications and orbifold universal coverings

Definition.

A pair (𝑆,𝜈) consisting of a Riemann surface 𝑆 and a ramification function 𝜈 :𝑆 →ℕ∗ (𝜈 =1 except for a discrete closed subset) is called a Riemann surface orbifold.

For regular branched covering 𝑝 :𝑆′ →𝑆, we can define 𝜈(𝑤) =𝑛(𝑧) for any 𝑧 ∈𝑓−1(𝑤), where 𝑣(𝑤) ⩾2 iff 𝑤 is a ramified point.

Definition.

A branched covering 𝑝 :𝑆′ →𝑆 is a universal covering for the Riemann surface orbifold (𝑆,𝜈) if

  • 𝑆′ is simply connected
  • the local degree at every point 𝑧 ∈𝑆′ equals 𝑣(𝑝(𝑧)).

In the case of a universal covering, the group Γ of deck transforms is called the fundamental group 𝜋1(𝑆,𝜈) of the orbifold.

Theorem.

Every Riemann surface orbifold (𝑆,𝜈) has a universal covering ˜𝑆𝜈 which is necessarily a regular covering, and unique up to a conformal isomorphism on 𝑆, except for the following cases

  • 𝑆 ≅ˆℂ with one ramified point
  • 𝑆 ≅ˆℂ with two ramified points and 𝜈(𝑤1) ≠𝜈(𝑤2).

See Problem E-3 and E-4 in Milnor, page 258, or examples.

Definition.

A branched covering 𝑓 :𝑆′ →𝑆 yield an orbifold covering map if for (𝑆′,𝜇),(𝑆,𝜈), 𝑛(𝑧)𝜇(𝑧)=𝑣(𝑓(𝑧))for all 𝑧 ∈𝑆′.

The universal covering ˜𝑆𝜈 is an orbifold covering with the trivial ramification.

If (𝑆,𝜈) with non-trivial ramification is a Riemann surface orbifold with a universal covering, then

  • 𝜒(𝑆,𝜈) >0 iff 𝜋1(𝑆,𝜈) is finite, of order 2/𝜒(𝑆,𝜈), and ˜𝑆𝜈 =ˆℂ

  • 𝜒(𝑆,𝜈) <0 iff 𝜋1(𝑆,𝜈) contains 𝐹2, and no abelian subgroup of finite index.
Lemma.

Bad orbifolds.

There is no universal covering for

  • ˆℂ with one ramified point
  • ˆℂ with two ramified points and 𝜈(𝑤1) ≠𝜈(𝑤2).

Proof.
Suppose 𝑓 :(𝑆,𝜇) →(ˆℂ,𝜈) is a universal covering, where 𝑆 is simply connected and 𝜇 is the trivial ramification

  • For the first case, the RH formula reads 𝜒(𝑆) =(2+1𝜈−1)deg⁡𝑓 =(1+1𝜈)deg⁡𝑓 >1, so 𝜒(𝑆) =2 and deg⁡𝑓 =2𝜈𝜈+1 can not be an integer.
  • For the second case, the RH formula reads 𝜒(𝑆) =(1𝜈1+1𝜈2)deg⁡𝑓 >0, so 𝜒(𝑆) =1,2 and deg⁡𝑓 =𝜒(𝑆)𝜈1𝜈2𝜈1+𝜈2. However, deg⁡𝑓 =∑𝑧∈𝑓−1(𝑤)𝑛(𝑧) =∑𝑧∈𝑓−1(𝑤)𝜈(𝑤) =#𝑓−1(𝑤)𝜈(𝑤), which means 𝜈𝑖 ∣deg⁡𝑓. For 𝜈1 >𝜈2, deg⁡𝑓𝜈1 =𝜒(𝑆)𝜈2𝜈1+𝜈2 <1 can not be an integer.
Existence of universal coverings.

Except for the bad orbifolds above, there is always a universal covering ˜𝑆𝜈 for any (𝑆,𝜈).

Proof.

  • If 𝑆 =ℂ,𝔻, let 𝑅 be the set of ramified points, fix 𝑧 ∈𝑆\𝑅, then 𝜋1(𝑆\𝑅,𝑧) = ∗𝑥∈𝑅ℤ[ℓ𝑥] is the free group generated by loops ℓ𝑥 around each 𝑥 ∈𝐴. Let 𝑁 be the normal subgroup generated by powers ℓ𝜈(𝑥)𝑥, and 𝑆′ be the covering space of 𝑆\𝑅 corresponding to 𝑁. Fill the punctures in 𝑆′, and the resulting surface is the universal covering.
  • If 𝑆 is Euclidean or hyperbolic in the usual sense, take the usual universal covering of 𝑆 and use the pull-back ramification: 𝜇(𝑧) :=𝜈(𝜋(𝑧)), and reduce to the first case.
  • If 𝑆 =ˆℂ with at most three ramified points, or with four ramified points of indices (2,2,2,2), the ramification indices are listed in the section orbifold Euler characteristic, the universal covering is either ˆℂ, ℂ or 𝔻.
  • If 𝑆 =ˆℂ with four or more ramified points and 𝜒(𝑆,𝜈) <0, the idea is to reduce to the second case. Take three ramified points, and find a simply connected orbifold covering (𝑆′,𝜈′) which is ramified only over these three points. Repeat the procedure until we get some (˜𝑆,𝜇) with ˜𝑆 Euclidean or hyperbolic. This can be done in finite many steps, since we eliminate all the indices of value 2 soon.
